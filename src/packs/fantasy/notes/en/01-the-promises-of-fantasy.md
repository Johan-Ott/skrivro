# The promises of fantasy

A reader who opens a fantasy novel wants to be taken somewhere else, and to trust that the place will hold. This is what you promise, and what the reader notices when you break it.

- **Wonder.** At least once per part, the reader should see something they have never seen: a city built inside a waterfall, magic that sounds like glass cracking. Wonder is the heart of the genre.
- **A world that holds together.** What was true in chapter three is true in chapter thirty. If fire cannot be tamed at the start, it cannot suddenly obey at the end.
- **Magic that costs.** The reader wants to feel that every spell takes something. Magic without a price becomes a shortcut, and shortcuts kill tension.
- **Stakes on two scales.** The world is at stake, but what makes the reader care is personal: a sister, a promise, a home.
- **A main character who grows.** The person standing there at the end is not the one we met at the start. The journey through the world is also a journey inward.
- **A what if that carries the book.** Every fantasy rests on an idea: what if memories could be sold, what if the gods died when no one prayed to them any more. The idea should be felt in every part of the book.
- **An ending that settles the big question.** The main conflict is resolved in this book, even in a series. Leave threads if you like, but not the central one.

**For urban fantasy** the same promises apply, plus one more: the ordinary city must feel real, so the hidden world beneath it becomes believable. The bus stop, the late-night corner shop, the neighbour nobody ever sees, any of them can hide something.

**Try this:** write your book's what if in a single sentence. If you can't, that is the first thing to solve.
