# Subgenres

Epic and urban fantasy, portal fantasy and low fantasy are in the guide Epic and urban fantasy. Romantasy has its own pack, Romantasy. Here are more, with promise, set-up and pitfall.

**Grimdark**

- The promise: a world without heroes, where choices cost and nobody is clean.
- Set-up: morally grey people, violence with consequences, victories that wound.
- Pitfall: darkness as a pose. Cruelty without consequence is just scenery.

**Cosy fantasy**

- The promise: low stakes, warmth and a community that heals.
- Set-up: a cafe, a bookshop, a village. The conflict is small but real.
- Pitfall: forgetting the conflict. Cosy without direction is only atmosphere.

**Sword and sorcery**

- The promise: a lone adventurer, short and hard action, danger from page to page.
- Set-up: one job, one price, one city or ruin.
- Pitfall: episodic with no arc. Let something happen to the hero.

**Mythic fantasy and folklore**

- The promise: tales and legends brought back to life, often from a specific culture.
- Set-up: trolls, forest spirits and mermaids are real, with rules of their own.
- Pitfall: picking the exotic without understanding it. Do your research.

**Young fantasy**

- The promise: a young protagonist, a school or a first journey, and a world that opens up.
- Set-up: short chapters, close viewpoint, clear friendship.
- Pitfall: childish simplification. Young readers can take darkness if it has meaning.

**Historical fantasy**

- The promise: a real era with a hidden magic.
- Set-up: the era's rules and daily life, magic that fits within them.
- Pitfall: the costume taking over. The magic should change history, not decorate it.

**Ask yourself:**

- Which subgenre is mine, and which am I drifting into by accident?
- Is it the world, the magic or the people the reader comes for?
- How much magic does my first chapter promise?
