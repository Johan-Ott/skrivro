# Examples: fantasy that does it well

Read them as craft: look for how they do it, not what happens. The sentences are our own.

- **The Lord of the Rings**, J.R.R. Tolkien (1954-55). Shows how a world gains depth when history, language and landscape lie behind every place the characters pass.
- **A Wizard of Earthsea**, Ursula K. Le Guin (1968). A magic system where a name and a price belong together, told in a language that is spare and exact.
- **Mistborn: The Final Empire**, Brandon Sanderson (2006). Magic with clear rules and limits, and how to build tension on the reader understanding them.
- **The Blade Itself**, Joe Abercrombie (2006). Shows how gray, ironic characters who contradict themselves bring grimdark to life.
- **Rivers of London**, Ben Aaronovitch (2011). A city as a character of its own, and dry humor in the middle of the supernatural.
- **The Brothers Lionheart**, Astrid Lindgren (1973). Shows how a fairy-tale world can carry seriousness about fear and courage, in a simple and exact language.

**Read like this:**

- What is the book's what-if, and where do you first notice it?
- What does the magic cost, and where is the price shown?
- Which moment of wonder do you remember?
