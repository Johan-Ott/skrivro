# Common pitfalls and revision

**Pitfalls**

- **The prologue as history lesson.** A thousand years of war before we meet a single person. Start with someone, and let the history come in piece by piece.
- **The chosen one without a choice.** A prophecy that does the work for the main character. Make the prophecy wrong, ambiguous, or something they must choose to fulfil.
- **Magic as last-minute rescue.** A new power awakens exactly when it's needed. Plant it early, and show what it costs.
- **Worldbuilding instead of story.** Thirty pages on the customs system. Keep it in your notes; the book only needs what affects the scene.
- **Invented words for ordinary things.** A special word for horse or coffee wears the reader out. Only invent words for what is truly new.
- **One people, one trait.** The whole nation is warlike, or wise. Give every culture internal conflict: rich and poor, believers and doubters.
- **Evil without a reason.** A dark lord who destroys for the sake of destruction. Give the antagonist a goal that can be understood.
- **Urban fantasy: a secret without logic.** Why has nobody noticed? Give the secrecy a mechanism and a price.

**Revision checklist**

- Cut explanations as a test. Read the scene without them. Is anything missing?
- Go through every invented name and word. Could any be replaced with an ordinary one?
- Ask in every scene with magic: is the cost visible here?
- Read the final battle next to your magic system note. Does any solution break a rule?
- Tag scenes with the Magic and The cost labels and filter them in Contents. Are they spread through the book, or bunched together?
- Read the first chapter aloud. Within a few pages, does a new reader know who this is about, where we are and what the character wants?
