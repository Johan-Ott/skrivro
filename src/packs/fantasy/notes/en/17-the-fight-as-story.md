# The fight as story

A fight is a scene like any other: someone wants something, something stands in the way, and something changes. What sets it apart is that the reader can tire of movements and miss the orientation. Write it as a story, not as choreography.

**The question before the blows.** What is at stake in this fight, and what changes when it ends? If the answer is "nothing, we get on" you can cut it to a few lines.

**One viewpoint, one feeling.** Stay close to one person. The reader needs to know what they see, feel and decide, not what everyone does. Write the chaos of the fight through what they have time to notice: a sound, a shadow, pain in the side.

**Orientation first, then movement.** The reader should know where the character stands, where the enemy is and what lies between before the blows begin. One sentence is enough. If the reader loses orientation, they lose the fight.

**Price and consequence.** Every fight costs something: an injury that stays, a friend, a weapon, trust. Show the consequence in the next scene, and let it affect the next fight. Without a price, the fights are interchangeable.

**Rhythm.** Short sentences and paragraphs when it moves fast. A longer breath when something stands still: in the second before, in the silence after.

**The solution should be the character's.** If the protagonist wins with something they have built up earlier, such as a choice, a skill or a relationship, the victory feels earned. If they win by luck or a new power, it feels cheap.

**Magic in the fight.** Let the magic follow its rules and have its price, in the middle of the worst. The price is strongest when they must pay it right then.

**Different kinds of fight.**

- **The duel:** two wills, few blows. Let each blow say something about the person.
- **The battle:** many people and one viewpoint. Show the whole through a single moment, not through a map.
- **The ambush:** surprise and confusion. Skip the lead-up.
- **The siege:** waiting, shortage and decisions. The fighting is a part, not the whole.
- **The flight:** movement and choice. Every corner is a decision.

**What you can skip.** Often it is enough to show the beginning, a turning moment and the end. The rest can be summed up in a sentence: "It took three hours".

**Try this:** write the outcome of the fight in one sentence, and what it costs. Then write the fight so that every paragraph moves it forward.
