# Examples: science fiction that does it well

Read them as craft: look for how they do it, not what happens. The sentences are our own.

- **Kallocain**, Karin Boye (1940). Shows how an idea about a society can be built out into a whole world, with consequences in daily life, language and feeling.
- **Aniara**, Harry Martinson (1956). A spaceship told as a sequence of poems, and proof that the genre can carry language and a question about humanity's place.
- **Dune**, Frank Herbert (1965). Shows how ecology, religion and politics are woven into a world that feels as alive as its people.
- **The Left Hand of Darkness**, Ursula K. Le Guin (1969). A what-if followed through a whole society, which still stays close to two people.
- **Neuromancer**, William Gibson (1984). A language and a setting that carry the whole idea, without explaining it.
- **The Martian**, Andy Weir (2011). Hard science fiction where problem-solving is the drama itself, and a voice keeps the reader hooked.

**Read like this:**

- What is the book's what-if, and when can you say it yourself?
- Which third-order consequence do you get to see?
- Where is the idea shown in action rather than in explanation?
