# Tropes and devices

A trope is a device the reader recognises and wants: the generation ship, the thinking machine, time travel. The trope is the promise, the execution is yours. The dystopia, first contact and the story after the catastrophe are subgenres and are in the guide Subgenres.

Each line: what it does, when it works, when it turns cliché.

- **The generation ship.** A ship that takes generations. _Works when_ the society aboard has evolved, with its own politics and beliefs. _Cliché when_ nobody has forgotten anything and it's all like Earth.
- **The thinking machine.** An AI with will. _Works when_ its goals are understandable but strange. _Cliché when_ it merely mirrors our own fear.
- **Time travel.** A change in the past. _Works when_ the rules are strict and the price holds. _Cliché when_ the paradox is solved with a shrug.
- **The clone and the copy.** A person who exists in several copies. _Works when_ the question of who is the self is taken seriously. _Cliché when_ the copy only exists to deliver a twist.
- **The space colony.** A new place, new rules. _Works when_ the place sets terms for the people. _Cliché when_ the setting is a desert with a new name.
- **The great threat from outside.** A fleet, a signal, an asteroid. _Works when_ people must choose between themselves. _Cliché when_ the whole world unites in three pages.
- **The inventor and the consequences.** Someone creates something that changes everything. _Works when_ the inventor doesn't grasp what they've done. _Cliché when_ they are a lone genius who causes it all.
- **Cryosleep.** Someone wakes in another time. _Works when_ the strangeness shows in the details of daily life and everything they knew is gone. _Cliché when_ it is only a convenient excuse to have a newcomer ask questions.

**Make it yours.** Change one condition, not the trope: who owns the technology, what it costs, where it happens, what people believe about it. If you build the book on one trope, break it once, on purpose.
