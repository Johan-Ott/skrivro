# Scale, time and distance

In science fiction distances are vast and time is long. That gives wonder, but also a practical problem: how do you write a story that a human being can follow across light years and centuries? Decide how you handle scale before you plan the plot, because it governs what a message, a journey and a wait cost.

**Three common choices.**

- **The shortcut.** Travel is fast, communication immediate. It gives pace and makes meetings possible. The price: distances mean little, and you must find other obstacles. Decide what is still far: power, truth, culture.
- **The limit.** Light is a limit, so journeys take years and messages too. It gives weight, where every farewell may be forever. The price: the plot is harder to hold together. Ask who is still there when the protagonist comes back.
- **The middle road.** Some things are fast but not everything. Perhaps people can travel but messages cannot, or the other way round. The most interesting thing comes out of the difference: who knows what, and how old is it.

**Keep the human at the center.** A scale of centuries or galaxies becomes a story only when it lands with a person. Begin with what the protagonist can see, touch and lose. Let the large show as consequences in the small: a price tag, an old friend who has died, a language that has changed.

**The price of time.**

- **Time dilation.** Whoever travels fast comes home to another time. Count: what has happened, who is alive, what is true now?
- **Old news.** Everything that arrives from far away is already history. Let the protagonist act on information that may have been true long ago.
- **Long jobs.** A build that takes a hundred years has many owners. Whose idea is it when it is finished?

**Count once.** Make a spreadsheet of the longest journeys and the most important distances: how long, who waits, what has time to happen. Then you find your gaps before the reader does.

**Ask yourself:** which scene becomes possible only because the distances are as they are? That is the scene that makes your scale something other than a backdrop.
