# Common pitfalls and revision

**Pitfalls**

- **The lecture.** An expert explains the technology for three pages. Break it up, cut it down, and let it happen inside a conflict.
- **Technology without consequences.** The world has teleportation, yet everyone still takes the bus. Ask what should have changed.
- **The present in a spacesuit.** Society is exactly like ours, with a bit of chrome. The future has other customs, words and problems.
- **The solution out of nowhere.** A new invention saves the day in the last chapter. Plant it early, or let the solution grow out of the rules.
- **Characters who only carry ideas.** Each one stands for an opinion. Give them their own goals, fears and contradictions.
- **Humans in disguise.** Aliens or machines that think and feel exactly like us, or a whole people with a single trait. Give the alien other senses, needs and values, grown out of how it came to be.
- **The machine that wants one thing.** The artificial intelligence that wants to become human, or wipe us out. Give it goals that follow from how it was built and what it was made for.
- **A future that dates.** Today's gadgets, only bigger. Write what the technology does, not what it looks like.
- **The jargon swamp.** Ten invented terms on one page. Use ordinary words wherever you can, and save new terms for what is truly new.
- **Forgotten rules.** Time travel had a cost in the first part, but not in the last. Keep track of the rules.

**Revision, in three passes**

Pass 1, the structure:

- Tag scenes with the Novum and Consequence labels and filter them in Contents. Does the idea run through the whole book, or vanish in the middle?
- Read the ending next to your novum note. Does the solution follow the rules?
- Make rough estimates for distances, times and quantities. Do the same numbers hold throughout the book?

Pass 2, the scenes:

- Ask of every chapter: what does this mean for an ordinary person in this world?
- Mark every passage that explains technology. Can it be cut in half, or moved into a scene with conflict?
- Read the first chapter as a new reader. Do you know who this is about, where we are and what is at stake?

Pass 3, the lines:

- Go through every invented term. Is each one needed, and does context explain it?
