# Subgenres

The promises of science fiction hold for all of them, but each subgenre has its own weight. Choose early: it settles the scale, the tone and how much science the book carries.

**Space opera**

- The promise: scale, pace, big conflicts and a crew on a ship.
- Set-up: empires, fleets and politics, with a small crew at the center.
- Pitfall: the scale becoming scenery. Ask what someone in the crew wants.

**Hard science fiction**

- The promise: the science holds and the solution can be worked out.
- Set-up: a problem and a person who solves it with knowledge.
- Pitfall: the numbers being the heroes. Give the problem a human face.

**The dystopia**

- The promise: a system to fight, and a person who sees it.
- Set-up: daily life under the order, a first breach of it.
- Pitfall: evil without a reason. Show why people go along.

**Cyberpunk**

- The promise: near future, technology and decay on the same street.
- Set-up: hackers, corporations, the body as interface.
- Pitfall: style over substance. Neon is not a question.

**First contact**

- The promise: a meeting with something truly alien.
- Set-up: understanding comes slowly, and every assumption is tested.
- Pitfall: the meeting being solved with a conversation. Let understanding cost.

**After the catastrophe**

- The promise: a world that fell, and what is worth rebuilding.
- Set-up: daily life, resources and new rules.
- Pitfall: everything being hunting and violence. Let something grow too.

**Climate fiction and near future**

- The promise: recognition and unease, a future we can see from here.
- Set-up: small changes that grow, and people adapting.
- Pitfall: preaching. Let the people be right and wrong.

**Ask yourself:**

- Which subgenre is mine, and which am I drifting into by accident?
- Which what-if carries the book, and can I say it in one sentence?
- Which consequence of my what-if have I not yet dared to follow?
