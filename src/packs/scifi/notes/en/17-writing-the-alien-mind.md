# Writing the alien mind

A stranger, an AI or a species that thinks differently is the genre's biggest draw and hardest job. The reader wants to meet something truly different, but must still be able to follow it. Here are ways to make it work.

**Start in the body and the needs.** How a being thinks comes out of what it is: what it needs, how it moves, which senses it has. A species that lives in the dark has no word for color. An AI that can be copied has no obvious "I". Write down three conditions and let everything else follow.

**Tell the alien from the merely odd.** Alien is logical but different. Odd is random. Always ask: why do they do that? If the answer is "because it is alien" it is no explanation.

**Let the misreading be the story.** The meeting works when both sides get it wrong, and the errors cost something. Let the protagonist believe they understand, act, and see what really happened.

**Three ways to show a different mind.**

- **Through choices.** Show what the being chooses when the alternatives are something we would not see as alternatives.
- **Through language.** A word with no equivalent, a grammar without tense, a greeting that is a question. Use it sparingly, and let the word matter to the plot.
- **Through what it doesn't care about.** The most alien thing is often what stirs nothing in them: death, ownership, loneliness.

**Avoid the comfortable shortcuts.**

- **Human with tentacles.** The same feelings and politics in a new costume.
- **The brain that is a computer.** An AI that is cold and logical for no reason. Give it a will that is understandable but not ours.
- **The all-knowing.** A being that knows everything becomes a deus ex machina. Give it gaps.
- **Translation without loss.** See the trope The translator.

**Ask whether the alien mirrors us.** The best meeting says something about humans: what we take for granted, what we would give up, what we cannot understand about ourselves. Write what your stranger shows about us in one sentence.
