# The promises of science fiction

Science fiction promises one thing above all: to take an idea seriously and follow it all the way. After each promise comes the price of breaking it.

- **A clear what if.** The book rests on a change to our world: what if sleep could be downloaded, what if a ship took five generations to arrive. After a few chapters the reader should be able to state the idea themselves. _Otherwise the reader doesn't know what the book is about._
- **Consequences, not just gadgets.** The technology is not the point. The point is what it does to people, power, love and work. _Without consequences the technology is scenery._
- **A logic that holds.** The world follows its own rules. If the speed of light is a limit at the start it is one at the end, or breaking it is the story itself. _If a rule bends for the plot's sake, the reader stops believing._
- **Strange and familiar at once.** The world should be strange enough to stir curiosity and familiar enough to say something about ours. The genre thinks in the gap between them. _Only strange is incomprehensible, only familiar is today with new names._
- **Wonder.** At some point the reader should lose their breath at the scale: an ocean under the ice, a city that thinks, a century passing in a single journey. _Without it the scale is never felt._
- **People at the centre.** The big idea is felt through a person with something to lose. Without that person the book is an essay. _Ideas without people become a lecture._
- **Credibility before precision.** The reader doesn't need the formulas, but should believe you have them. One confident detail beats a long explanation. _Explain too much and the reader skips pages._
- **A question that lingers.** The best of the genre leaves the reader with a thought about their own time. _Without it the book is forgotten with the last page._

**Breaking one on purpose.** Readers forgive a broken convention when they can tell it was a choice. Break one promise at a time: a book can be humanly closer than technically, and soft science fiction gives up precision but must keep its inner logic. Keep the rest rock solid.

**The subgenres promise different things.** Space opera promises pace and scale, hard science fiction that the science holds, the dystopia a system to fight, and first contact a meeting with something truly alien. The guide Subgenres goes through them and more. Decide which promise your book makes on page one, and keep it.

**Try this:** write your book's what if in one sentence, and the most important consequence for ordinary people in one more.
