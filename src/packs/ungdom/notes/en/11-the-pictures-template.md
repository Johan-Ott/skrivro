# The pictures (template)

For chapter books. Rename the note to the chapter or the picture, like "Chapter 2, the dragon in the garage". In a chapter book the pictures carry part of the story, often with a picture on every spread or every other spread for the youngest readers. Make one for each picture the story needs, and put the Picture here label where it belongs in the text.

If you publish with a publisher, it is usually the publisher who chooses and pays the illustrator. Send the manuscript without pictures of your own, and with as few illustration notes as possible: only where the text can't be understood without the picture. The rest of the template is for you, or for the conversation with the illustrator later on.

**Where in the text:** chapter and passage, and whether the picture is large, small or fills a whole spread.

**The moment:** exactly what is happening in the picture. Choose the most exciting, funny or emotional moment, ideally one the text doesn't linger on.

**What the picture shows that the text doesn't say:** a facial expression, a detail in the background, a joke only the picture has.

**What the text no longer needs to say:** descriptions the picture takes over, so you can cut them.

**The characters:** who is in it, and what they look like. Keep their appearance the same throughout the book.

**The mood:** light and feeling. Safe, scary, mischievous?

**Something for the reader to spot:** a small thing to find, like a cat that appears in every picture.

**The illustration note in the manuscript:** if one is needed, write it in a single sentence.

**For the illustrator:** what you want them to know, and what they are free to invent.
