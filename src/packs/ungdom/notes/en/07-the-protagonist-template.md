# The protagonist (template)

Rename the note to the protagonist's name. The template works for every age; in a chapter book one sentence per field is often enough.

**Age and school year:** how old they are, and which year or stage of school. A year or two older than the reader.

**The narrator:** "I" or "she", "he" or "they", past or present tense? First person is common in YA, often in the present tense. Chapter books are written in both first and third person.

**Wants:** what they chase when the book begins, said the way they would say it. "I just want everything to go back to how it was."

**Needs:** what they really have to learn or dare to do, which they don't know yet.

**The problem:** the book's big problem, as it looks to them. Why is it enormous at exactly that age?

**Why the adults can't fix it:** they don't know, they don't understand, they're part of the problem, or the protagonist has promised not to tell.

**The strength:** what they're good at, which will solve the problem in the end. Plant it early.

**The flaw:** what makes them get things wrong, like stubbornness, shyness or a temper.

**The fear:** what they would least like anyone to find out.

**The voice:** three phrases or ways of thinking that are theirs alone. How do they sound when they're angry, happy, embarrassed?

**Home:** who they live with, what it's like there, and what they miss.

**What they do themselves at the end:** the decisive step, by their own means. What does it cost them?

**The change:** how they are different on the last page, without anyone having to say so.
