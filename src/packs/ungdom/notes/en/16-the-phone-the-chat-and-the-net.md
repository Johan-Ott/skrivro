# The phone, the chat and the net in the text

Young readers live partly on the screen, so a book that pretends it does not exist feels old. But how it is shown dates fast and can get in the way. Here is how to make it work.

**Decisions to make once**

- **Messages as running text or as lines of their own?** Short posts can stand as separate lines. Long threads get tiring to read. Choose one form and keep to it, and check in the preview that the export handles it.
- **The names of services.** Invent your own or write "the chat" and "the feed". Real app names date fast. What readers know today may be gone by the time the book is out.
- **Emojis and abbreviations.** Use them sparingly. A single short answer often says more than a line of symbols.

**What the screen does for the story**

- **The gap between channels.** What is said in the chat is not what is said in the corridor. Use the difference.
- **Waiting.** Three dots moving, a message read but not answered. It is tension the reader feels in their gut.
- **Exposure.** Anything written can be shown to everyone, which raises the stakes of every secret.
- **The other self.** The profile that shows someone the protagonist is not, or does not dare to be.

**Pitfalls**

- **The phone as a shortcut.** No one can be lost or trapped if everyone can call. Decide why it does not help: the battery, the signal, not wanting anyone to know.
- **The screen as explanation.** Do not let a chat tell what has happened instead of showing it.
- **The moral lecture.** The net is neither evil nor good. Show what it does to these particular people.

Try this: take the screens out of a scene. Does it work just as well? Then the screen does nothing there. Cut it.
