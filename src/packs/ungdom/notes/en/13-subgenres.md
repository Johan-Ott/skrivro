# Subgenres

The age bands are in Age bands: voice and length. Here are the genres within them. The promises to young readers hold for all, but the weight shifts. Choose early.

**The realistic**

- The promise: a problem the reader recognises, told truthfully and close.
- Set-up: an everyday world, a change and a choice. The stakes are personal but big.
- Pitfall: the topic matters more than the person. Start with her.

**Love and crushes**

- The promise: butterflies, uncertainty and the first time something matters a lot. The younger the reader, the more glances and the less body.
- Set-up: two people, an obstacle and a choice. The friendships around them carry.
- Pitfall: the crush solves everything. Let the person also have a life of their own.

**Fantasy for young readers**

- The promise: a world with rules, a gift that costs and a friendship that is tested.
- Set-up: a clear start in everyday life, a short way into the magic, chapters that pull.
- Pitfall: long world-building before the plot. Show the world through what the person wants.

**Dystopia for young readers**

- The promise: a system the reader recognises from their own school or feed, and a main character who can say no.
- Set-up: a small rebellion that grows, with choices that cost.
- Pitfall: an explanation scene. Let the system show in daily life.

**Suspense and horror for young readers**

- The promise: fear on safe ground, with an ending the reader can handle.
- Set-up: a growing threat, friends who believe or don't, and a showdown the main character decides.
- Pitfall: forgetting the age. Fear is strongest when it comes from something the reader knows.

**Humour and school satire**

- The promise: laughter that doesn't joke away what matters.
- Set-up: a voice that carries, exaggeration and a tender core.
- Pitfall: jokes at the characters' expense. Let the humour come from them.

**Ask yourself:**

- Which subgenre is mine, and which am I drifting into by accident?
- Who am I writing for, and what do they want from the first chapter?
- Which age am I writing for, and does the voice sound like someone that age?
