# Checklist for the first draft

Read the draft as if you were the reader, at the age you're writing for. Then check:

- Do you know exactly which age band the book is for, and is the length roughly right?
- Is the protagonist a year or two older than the reader?
- Does the first page show someone who wants something, and something that rubs?
- Does the protagonist take the decisive step, rather than a parent, a teacher or chance?
- Does every chapter have a goal, an obstacle and a hook at the end?
- Is there a long stretch without the Hook label? Look there first.
- Does the protagonist have a friend or a group with wills of their own, not just people who agree?
- Are the adults believable, yet out of the way for good reasons when it counts?
- Does the dialogue sound like young people of that age, without becoming an adult's impression of them?
- Does the reader get to laugh or breathe out somewhere, even in a serious book?
- Are the hard themes, tagged Difficult theme, handled truthfully and with respect for the reader's age?
- Does the book end with hope, and has the protagonist changed?
- In a chapter book: is there a Picture here where the text rests, and does the text hold up when read aloud?
