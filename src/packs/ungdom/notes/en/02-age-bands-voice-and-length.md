# Age bands: voice and length

Decide the reader's age first. It sets the length, the language, the themes and how the protagonist thinks. As a rule, readers like to read about someone a year or two older than themselves. The figures below are guidelines, not rules: publishers and series have their own, and the Swedish market often runs shorter than the English-language one.

**Chapter book, ages 6 to 9**

- **Length:** often 3,000 to 15,000 words. The first books a child reads alone sit at the lower end. Chapters of 500 to 1,500 words.
- **Protagonist:** 7 to 10 years old.
- **Voice:** short sentences, everyday words, lots of dialogue and action. One new word at a time, ideally explained by context. A readability score helps; in Sweden the LIX index is common, and books for the youngest often score around 20 to 25.
- **Story:** one clear question, one or two side characters, humor and a safe ending. Pictures often carry part of the story.
- **Two readers:** the youngest often have the book read aloud by an adult, the older ones read it themselves. The text has to work both aloud and in silence.

**Middle grade, ages 9 to 12**

- **Length:** often 20,000 to 50,000 words. Swedish middle-grade books often fall between 20,000 and 40,000; in the English-language market 30,000 to 55,000 is common, and fantasy runs longer. Chapters of 1,000 to 2,500 words.
- **Protagonist:** 10 to 13 years old.
- **Voice:** close and direct, often first person. Friendship, family and finding courage are central. The protagonist sees the world but doesn't fully understand it.
- **Story:** one main plot and one or two subplots. Hard things can be there, but the ending should give hope. Romance goes no further than butterflies and glances.
- **Structure:** Middle-grade novel, sixteen chapters of just under 2,000 words with a subplot at home.

**Young adult, ages 13 and up**

- **Length:** often 40,000 to 80,000 words. Swedish YA novels often fall between 40,000 and 65,000; English-language ones more often 50,000 to 90,000, and fantasy more. Chapters of 1,500 to 3,500 words.
- **Two groups:** publishers often separate younger YA, for roughly 12 to 15, from older YA, for 15 and up. The older group can take darker subjects and is read by many adults too.
- **Protagonist:** 14 to 18 years old.
- **Voice:** intense, distinct and honest, with an inner life at least as important as the events. First person present tense is common, but close third person works just as well. Identity, love, loyalty and becoming yourself.
- **Story:** higher stakes and more complex choices. Themes can be dark and the ending can be open, but not hopeless.
- **Structure:** each step in Young adult novel often becomes two or three chapters.

**Voice at any age**

- Write the way the protagonist thinks, not the way an adult explains.
- Avoid slang that dates within a year. Find how your character speaks instead.
- Choose the narrator and the tense once, and stick to them.
- Read aloud. If a sentence trips you up, it will trip the reader up too.
