# Age bands: voice and length

Decide the reader's age first. It sets the length, the language, the themes and how the protagonist thinks. As a rule, readers like to read about someone a year or two older than themselves.

**Chapter book, ages 6 to 9**

- **Length:** often 2,000 to 12,000 words, chapters of 500 to 1,000 words.
- **Protagonist:** 7 to 10 years old.
- **Voice:** short sentences, everyday words, lots of dialogue and action. One new word at a time, ideally explained by context.
- **Story:** one clear question, one or two side characters, humour and a safe ending. Pictures often carry part of the story.

**Middle grade, ages 9 to 12**

- **Length:** often 25,000 to 55,000 words, chapters of 1,500 to 2,500 words.
- **Protagonist:** 10 to 13 years old.
- **Voice:** close and direct, often first person. Friendship, family and finding courage are central. The protagonist sees the world but doesn't fully understand it.
- **Story:** one main plot and one or two subplots. Hard things can be there, but the ending should give hope.

**Young adult, ages 13 and up**

- **Length:** often 45,000 to 80,000 words, chapters of 2,000 to 3,500 words.
- **Protagonist:** 14 to 18 years old.
- **Voice:** intense, distinct and honest, with an inner life at least as important as the events. Identity, love, loyalty and becoming yourself.
- **Story:** higher stakes and more complex choices. Themes can be dark and the ending can be open, but not hopeless.

**Voice at any age**

- Write the way the protagonist thinks, not the way an adult explains.
- Avoid slang that dates within a year. Find how your character speaks instead.
- Read aloud. If a sentence trips you up, it will trip the reader up too.
