# Examples: books for young readers that do it well

Read them as craft: look for how they do it, not what happens. The sentences are our own.

- **My Friend Percy, Buffalo Bill and Me**, Ulf Stark (1984). Shows how a child's voice can be dry, warm and funny in the same sentence, without a grown-up explaining.
- **Mio, My Son**, Astrid Lindgren (1954). A child at the centre who decides their own way, in a world that is clear and easy to believe.
- **Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone**, J.K. Rowling (1997). A world laid out through what the main character learns, chapter by chapter, with chapter endings that pull you on.
- **The Hunger Games**, Suzanne Collins (2008). Pace, short chapters and a present-tense voice that makes every choice physical.
- **Speak**, Laurie Halse Anderson (1999). A hard theme shown from the inside, with short passages and a voice that is dry and sharp.
- **Sandor slash Ida**, Sara Kadefors (2001). A crush in the school's present tense, where small details carry more than big words.

**Read like this:**

- What does the main character want on page one?
- Where do the chapters end, and why do you read on?
- How close does the book go to the hard thing, and what does it do so the reader isn't left alone?
