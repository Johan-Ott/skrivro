# Short chapters with hooks

Every chapter is a small promise: something happens here, and something will happen next.

- **Come in late.** Start in the middle of the action. Not "Ella woke up and brushed her teeth", but "Ella had three minutes before the bus left, and her phone was gone."
- **One goal per chapter.** The protagonist wants something in this particular chapter: to find the cat, to make the team, to dodge a conversation.
- **An obstacle.** Something stands in the way, and the protagonist does something about it. They act; events don't just wash over them.
- **Leave early.** End the chapter as soon as the important thing has happened, before anyone has time to explain.
- **The hook.** Finish on something that makes the reader turn the page. Tag chapters with the Hook label and check that no long stretch goes without one.

**Five kinds of hook**

- **The question:** "Who had written her name on the board?"
- **The surprise:** someone turns up, something breaks, a secret leaks.
- **The decision:** "She was going. Tonight."
- **The danger:** something threatens, and we don't know how it will turn out.
- **The feeling:** a moment that hurts or delights, and whose consequences the reader wants to see.

**Immediacy in the scene**

- Stay in the protagonist's body and senses. What does the corridor smell like, how does their stomach feel before the test?
- Let dialogue do the work. Young readers skip long descriptions but read every line of dialogue.
- Show feelings through action: they slam the locker, ignore the phone, leave their dinner.

Try this: take a chapter and cut the first and last paragraph. Does it now start in motion and end on a hook? Those are often exactly the paragraphs that explain too much.
