# Typical scenes

These scenes recur because readers recognize them from their own lives. No book has all of them. In fantasy and horror they often take another shape: the camp, the ship, the forbidden forest.

**The first day somewhere new.** New school, new team, new home. Show three things the protagonist notices that an insider would not. _Works when:_ the scene raises the question of who they become here. _Pitfall:_ a tour where someone explains the rules.

**The cafeteria or recess.** Where you sit says who you are. _Works when:_ a small choice, which table and whom you ask, costs something. _Pitfall:_ a whole scene of jokes with no consequence.

**The advice nobody takes.** Someone says something the protagonist should have taken to heart. _Works when:_ it is planted early, in passing, so they can remember it when it is too late.

**The unfinished kiss or conversation.** The flutter before anything has happened. _Works when:_ the scene stays in the body: hands, breath, what is not said. _Pitfall:_ explaining what both feel.

**The night no adult knows about.** The party, the woods, the roof. The line moves, often a little at a time. _Works when:_ the first step is easy and the third hard to back out of. _Pitfall:_ the moral lecture. The consequence is enough.

**The lie to a parent.** It goes well the first time. _Works when:_ the parent is reasonable, so the lie feels like a choice and not a shield against a foolish adult.

**The fight in the group.** Someone says what cannot be taken back. _Works when:_ both have a point. _Pitfall:_ a clear right and wrong.

**The news.** A parent, a teacher or a chat that changes things. _Works when:_ the body reacts before the thought. The reader should feel it before the protagonist understands it.

**The quiet afterwards.** The night, the bike ride, the room with the door shut. _Works when:_ the protagonist gets a single clear sign of what they now understand, without saying it outright.

**The last conversation.** With the friend, the parent or the one they hurt. _Works when:_ it happens between two people where at least one of them has been changed by the book.

**For younger readers.** Swap the party for the class trip, the birthday or the sleepover, and the kiss for a friend who no longer wants to play. The same building blocks and the same stakes, in a smaller world.
