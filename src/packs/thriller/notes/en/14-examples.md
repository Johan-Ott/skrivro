# Examples: thrillers that do it well

Read them as craft: look for how they do it, not what happens. The sentences are our own.

- **The Day of the Jackal**, Frederick Forsyth (1971). Shows how a clock and a clear plan can carry a whole book, even when the reader knows what is at stake.
- **The Silence of the Lambs**, Thomas Harris (1988). A conversation can be an entire scene: every line has a goal and a price.
- **The Spy Who Came in from the Cold**, John le Carré (1963). Shows how a spy novel can build tension from silence, mistrust and small assignments, without chases.
- **The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo**, Stieg Larsson (2005). Lets several threads and a slow pace build toward a pressure that stays in the mind.
- **Easy Money**, Jens Lapidus (2006). Several viewpoints with goals of their own, and a language that makes the setting feel real.
- **Gone Girl**, Gillian Flynn (2012). Shows how a home and a marriage can be made a threatening setting, and how voice and pace keep the reader hooked.

**Read like this:**

- Where does the danger begin, and how do you notice it?
- What is the clock, and when does it get shorter?
- What is the protagonist forced to choose?
