# Subgenres

The promises of the thriller hold for all of them, but the threat lives in different places. Choose early: it settles the villain, the clock and how close the reader gets.

**The action thriller**

- The promise: an external threat, a villain with resources and a chase across several places.
- Pitfall: choreography without feeling. Let every scene cost something.

**The conspiracy thriller**

- The promise: someone hides something, and the protagonist knows too much.
- Pitfall: paranoia without grounds. Let the reader see what is actually hidden.

**The psychological thriller**

- The promise: the threat is close, at home, in a relationship or in the protagonist's memory. It has its own guide.
- The line to the mystery: a mystery asks who, a thriller asks whether anyone survives. If the book mostly asks who, it belongs in Crime.
- Pitfall: the reveal being a diagnosis instead of a choice.

**The spy thriller**

- The promise: nobody is who they say, and loyalty costs.
- Pitfall: jargon and organisation charts. Let one person carry it.

**The political thriller**

- The promise: power as a threat, and a small person in the way.
- Pitfall: a debate book. The hero needs a personal reason.

**The survival thriller**

- The promise: one person, one threat and few resources in a closed setting.
- Pitfall: the threat vanishing midway. Keep it present.

**The medical and tech thriller**

- The promise: a technical threat that an expert must stop.
- Pitfall: the expert's lecture. Show the technology in action.

**The legal thriller.** The truth is tested in a courtroom. Pitfall: law as lecture, so make it drama.

**Ask yourself:**

- Which subgenre is mine, and which am I drifting into by accident?
- What happens if the protagonist fails, and when?
- Where does the threat live in my book?
