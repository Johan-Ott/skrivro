# Pitfalls and revision

**Common pitfalls**

- **The passive main character.** Things happen to them, but they do nothing. From the second part on, they should make decisions that drive the plot, even bad ones.
- **The stupid villain.** A villain who blunders only because the plot needs it makes the win cheap. Let them lose because of the main character's wits.
- **The forgotten clock.** A deadline mentioned at the start and never again. Remind, and shrink it.
- **Flat stakes.** The same danger in chapter three as in chapter twenty. Each part should have more at stake than the last.
- **Rescued by chance.** Luck may get the main character into trouble, never out of it.
- **The obvious way out.** The reader wonders why they do not call the police, run or tell someone. Close every such exit on the page, with a reason the reader accepts: the police are involved, no one believes them, it would cost someone else their life.
- **Forgetting technology.** Phones, cameras and tracking exist. Let them work as they do in real life, and build the obstacles around them instead of pretending they are not there.
- **A twist for its own sake.** A surprise that was never planted, or that makes everything before it pointless, feels like cheating.
- **Action without feeling.** A chase where the reader does not know what the main character thinks and fears is just choreography.
- **The repetitive middle.** Chase, escape, chase, escape. Each sequence should change something: new knowledge, a new ally, a new loss.
- **The long aftermath.** Once the threat is over the book is nearly done. One or two short chapters are enough.

**Revision, in this order**

- Read every chapter ending in a row. Does each one pull, and do they vary?
- Read the villain's plan alongside the manuscript. Does the villain do anything against the plan or their own interest?
- Track the clock: mark every place time is mentioned. Are there long gaps?
- Check each major twist: where is the seed planted?
- Cut explanations, backstory and trips from A to B that carry no tension.
- Give the manuscript to a test reader and ask them to mark where they put the book down.
