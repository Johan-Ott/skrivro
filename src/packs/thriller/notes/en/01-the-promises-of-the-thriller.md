# The promises of the thriller

A mystery asks who. A thriller asks whether, and how, in time. The reader wants to be afraid of someone, and to hold their breath until the last page.

- **Danger from page one.** Not necessarily an explosion, but a sense that something is off. A car parked too long, an email that should not exist.
- **A main character to worry about.** Capable enough to fight, vulnerable enough to lose. The reader should know exactly what they stand to lose.
- **A clever opponent.** The villain has a plan, and it works for a long time. The stronger the villain, the bigger the win.
- **A ticking clock.** A deadline the reader can feel and count down. Every chapter there is less time left.
- **Rising stakes.** From a job to a life to a child, or from one person to a whole city. The stakes must never drop.
- **Twists that reframe.** At least one twist that makes the reader see everything so far in a new light, and that could be spotted in hindsight.
- **The main character decides it.** In the showdown, their choices and skills are what count. No rescue from outside at the last second.
- **Pace.** Short chapters, scenes that end in motion, and few pauses that do not carry tension of their own.

**Test your manuscript:** can you say in one sentence what happens if the main character fails, and when? If the answer is vague, the clock and the stakes are not clear enough yet.
