# The promises of the thriller

A mystery asks who did it. A thriller asks whether the main character can stop it in time, and what it will cost. The reader wants to be afraid of someone, and to hold their breath until the last page. After each promise comes the price of breaking it.

- **Danger from page one.** Not necessarily an explosion, but a sense that something is off. A car parked too long, an email that should not exist. _Without it the reader has time to put the book down._
- **A main character to worry about.** Capable enough to fight, vulnerable enough to lose. The reader should know exactly what they stand to lose. _If they are flawless or indifferent, nothing is at stake._
- **A clever opponent.** The villain has a plan, and it works for a long time. The stronger the villain, the bigger the win. _A stupid villain makes the win cheap._
- **A ticking clock.** A deadline the reader can feel and count down. Every chapter there is less time left. _Without a clock, tension turns into a chase without direction._
- **Rising stakes.** From a job to a life to a child, or from one person to a whole city. The stakes must never drop. _If they sink, so does the middle._
- **Twists that reframe.** At least one twist that makes the reader see everything so far in a new light, and that could be spotted in hindsight. _A twist that isn't planted feels like cheating._
- **The main character decides it.** In the showdown, their choices and skills are what count. No rescue from outside at the last second. _If help comes from outside, the showdown belongs to someone else._
- **Pace.** Short chapters, scenes that end in motion, and few pauses that do not carry tension of their own. _Long pauses without pressure loosen the grip._

**Breaking one on purpose.** Readers forgive a broken convention when they can tell it was a choice. Break one promise at a time: a thriller without a skilled villain can carry if the threat is the system, and a slow thriller can gain depth if every scene still carries a question. Keep the rest rock solid.

**Choose your kind of thriller.** The promises hold for all of them, but the threat lives in different places. The guide Subgenres goes through the action thriller, the conspiracy thriller, the psychological thriller and more. The structure and the templates work for all: only swap what the villain, the plan and the clock are.

**Test your manuscript:** can you say in one sentence what happens if the main character fails, and when? If the answer is vague, the clock and the stakes are not clear enough yet.
