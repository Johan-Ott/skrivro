# Tropes and devices

A trope is a device the reader recognizes and wants: the bomb with a timer, the person you trusted, the chase in the last chapter. The trope is the promise, the execution is yours.

- **The ticking bomb.** _Works when_ time shrinks and every attempt costs minutes. _Cliché when_ it stops on 00:01 out of habit.
- **The wrong man accused.** _Works when_ the evidence convinces and nobody can help. _Cliché when_ the police are simply stupid.
- **The traitor on the team.** _Works when_ the signs show in hindsight and the traitor had a reason. _Cliché when_ it is always the kindest-seeming one.
- **Back in the game.** _Works when_ they have something to lose and a skill that costs. _Cliché when_ they are invincible.
- **The conspiracy goes high.** _Works when_ the protagonist knows little and must choose whom to trust. _Cliché when_ it turns out to go all the way to the president.
- **The family as hostage.** _Works when_ it forces the protagonist to choose between the mission and their own. _Cliché when_ it only raises the stakes and the victim has no will of their own.
- **The villain explains the plan.** _Works when_ the conversation is an interrogation with a hidden agenda. _Cliché when_ it only buys time for the rescue.
- **Knows too much.** _Works when_ they don't at first understand what they know. _Cliché when_ the memory returns exactly when needed.
- **The chase.** _Works when_ each leg changes and costs something. _Cliché when_ it is filler between two conversations.
- **The witness who must be kept alive.** _Works when_ the witness has their own reasons not to cooperate and their own fear. _Cliché when_ they are a packed bag to be moved between two shootouts.
- **A stranger in a strange land.** _Works when_ the unknown is an obstacle in every scene: the language, the laws, who to ask. _Cliché when_ a local guide explains everything.
- **The informant.** _Works when_ they risk more than the protagonist and are not fully honest. _Cliché when_ they die just before the key name.

**Make it yours.** Change one condition, not the trope: who carries the bomb, what the clock really measures, where the traitor sits, what the protagonist thinks they know. If you build the book on one trope, break it once, on purpose.
