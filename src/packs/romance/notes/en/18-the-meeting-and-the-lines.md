# The meeting and the lines

The reader waits for the first meeting and for every scene where the two are in the same room. It happens in what they say and don't say.

**The first meeting should be a promise.** It need not be love at first sight. It should show what they are to each other: obstacle, mirror, riddle, temptation. Give them each a goal in the scene, and let the goals collide.

**The meeting should cost something.** A line that wounds, a moment that gives away more than intended, a favor one does not want to owe. Then the reader has something to carry to the next scene.

**The banter.** Quick lines between two people are the genre's hallmark. It works when:

- **Both are quick in their own way.** A joke that comes from the character, not from the author.
- **Something lies under the joke.** One teases because she is afraid. The other answers because he wants her to stay.
- **It costs to stop.** Every quick line keeps them in the room.
- **It is broken.** By a silence, an honest sentence, a look. Then the worth of the rest shows.

**Subtext.** The most important thing is rarely said straight. "Want some coffee?" means "stay". "It's late" means "I don't want to go". Write down what they really mean, then write what is said. Sometimes the reader gets to be the only one who knows.

**The voices should differ.** Read only the lines one person says. Can you hear who it is without the tag? If not, the voices are too alike. Give each their own rhythm, their own words, their own way of avoiding.

**The meaning of touch.** The first time they touch is a scene. Let it come early enough for the reader to notice, and late enough that it matters. Small things carry: a hand on the back, a stray hair brushed away, a fingertip that stays.

**What they don't say.** What may never be said is the obstacle. Let the reader see the silence: what is almost said, the topic avoided, how a "we" becomes "you and me". When it is finally said it is a high point.

**Others in the room.** Side characters, friends and family can sharpen the tension: someone sees, someone teases, someone understands more than the couple. Use them, but don't lose the couple from view.

**Try this:** take a scene with two lines you find too kind. Rewrite them so each wants something the other can't give.
