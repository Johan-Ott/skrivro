# Pitfalls and revision

The most common pitfalls, and what to do about them.

- **The misunderstanding.** One honest conversation would have solved the conflict. Root it in fear, loyalty or a genuinely clashing goal.
- **Insta-love.** They fall without getting to know each other. Let the reader see why these two.
- **The perfect love interest.** Flawless is boring. Give them a wound and an arc of their own.
- **The unlikeable hero.** Coldness and sarcasm are fine, but cruelty without remorse makes the reader stop rooting.
- **A late meeting.** The couple should meet early. Two chapters of backstory before they see each other is too much.
- **The sagging middle.** If nothing new happens between them in the middle, add outside pressure: a deadline, an ex, a job on the line.
- **An ending that rushes.** The break-up takes five chapters and the reconciliation one page. Give the reconciliation time, and let both apologise if both were wrong.
- **Side characters who are only an audience.** The best friend needs a life, and sometimes an opinion that grates.

**Revision, in this order**

- Tag each scene Spark, Tension, Closeness or The wall and see if the pattern builds to the midpoint and then falls towards the break-up.
- Read only one lead's chapters, then only the other's: does each have a whole arc?
- Read the meeting, the midpoint and the ending in a row: do they connect?
- Read every chapter ending: does it pull you on?
- Check that the grand gesture answers the wound you showed at the start.
- In feel-good: tag Warmth and Humour and make sure no long stretch lacks both.
- Read the dialogue aloud, and cut anything that explains a feeling the scene already shows.
