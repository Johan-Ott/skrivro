# The craft of the love story

A love story works when two people have to change in order to love each other. Build everything on that.

- **Outer and inner obstacles.** The outer one keeps them apart at first: the job, the distance, a competition. The inner one keeps them apart for real: the fear, the wound, the lie. The outer one often resolves around the middle, the inner one only at the end.
- **The lie about love.** Both believe something false. "Everyone who says they will stay, leaves." "I am only worth something when I am useful." The relationship is what forces them to see it.
- **The other as a mirror.** The love interest should be exactly the person who challenges the lead's lie. She who never asks for help meets him who always offers it.
- **Slow burn.** Build the tension in small steps, and let each step cost something. A joke, an honest sentence, a hand on the back, a "stay". The reader should want them to kiss long before they do.
- **Two points of view.** With dual POV the reader sees both sides of every misreading and every longing. Switch when the other has something important to show, not just because it is their turn. The voices should sound different.
- **Tropes with intent.** Enemies to lovers, fake dating, second chance, only one bed, grumpy and sunshine. Readers look for them. Pick the one that forces out your characters' wounds, deliver what the trope promises and give it a turn of your own.
- **The break-up must make sense.** It grows from their fears, not from a misunderstanding a text message would have fixed. The reader should understand both, and still want to shake them.
- **The gesture fits the wound.** The grand gesture is not the biggest, it is the right one. She who believes no one stays needs to see him stay when it is hardest.
- **Feel-good: the place as a third character.** In feel-good the community has its own arc. What saves the place and what brings the couple together should ideally be the same thing.
