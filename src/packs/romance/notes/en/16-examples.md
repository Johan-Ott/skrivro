# Examples: romance that does it well

Read them as craft: look for how they do it, not what happens. The sentences are our own.

- **Pride and Prejudice**, Jane Austen (1813). Shows how conversation can be the love scene, and how a story gets its pull from what two people believe about each other.
- **Överenskommelser**, Simona Ahrnstedt (2010). A historical romance in Swedish where class and desire set the rules, and two people who want more than their time allows.
- **Bridget Jones's Diary**, Helen Fielding (1996). Humour that comes from a voice and a rule that collides with reality, in diary form.
- **The Hating Game**, Sally Thorne (2016). Builds tension between two people in one room, with lines that carry the attraction.
- **The Kiss Quotient**, Helen Hoang (2018). Gives both in the couple their own way of seeing the world, and lets closeness grow from it.
- **Red, White & Royal Blue**, Casey McQuiston (2019). Writes a large outer stage so that it keeps pressing on the relationship.
- **Beach Read**, Emily Henry (2020). Puts two people with different crafts on the same ground, so that work and love mirror each other.

**Read like this:**

- Where do you first feel the attraction, and what in the scene creates it?
- Which is the first obstacle you believe?
- How long before the couple have something true to say to each other?
