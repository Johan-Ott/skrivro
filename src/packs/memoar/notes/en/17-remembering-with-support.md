# Remembering with support

Memory is not a recording. It is redrawn each time we retrieve it, and we usually remember the feeling better than the details. A memoir that rests on memory alone becomes both thinner and more exposed than it needs to be.

**Start with what can be checked.**

- **The timeline.** Write year, month and place for what happened. Check against documents: report cards, postcards, receipts, hospital letters, moving notices.
- **Pictures.** Photos, films, the school yearbook. Look at what is in them and what is missing. What are people wearing, who stands next to whom?
- **Letters and messages.** Your own and others'. They show how you sounded then, which is worth more than how you remember sounding.
- **Witnesses.** Ask those who were there. Let them tell it first, and don't read out your version. The differences are interesting in themselves.
- **Public sources.** Newspaper archives, weather records, TV listings, registers. They help you place a day.

**Let the senses help.** Music from the time, a smell, a place you visit. Write freely for ten minutes without editing, and use what remains as raw material.

**Tell three kinds of memory apart.**

- **What you know.** Confirmed by documents or several people.
- **What you remember clearly.** Your picture, but no one else can confirm it.
- **What you reconstruct.** You don't know exactly what was said, but you know roughly how it was.

All three can become scenes, but be honest about the difference in the author's note or in the text: "I don't remember what she said, but I remember that it ended with me leaving."

**Dialogue.** Reproducing a conversation word for word decades later is not possible. The reader understands. Write what you remember of the content and tone, and keep to what it was possible to say. Say in the author's note that the lines are reconstructed.

**Memories that turn out wrong.** Sometimes you find that you remembered wrongly. Then you have been given a new scene: how could I believe that, and what does it say about me?

**Sensitive memories.** If you go into trauma, write at the pace you can manage, take breaks and have someone to talk to. The book is not made better by your getting hurt.

**In Skrivro.** Collect the evidence in Sources, with how certain it is. Mark scenes with Check facts until you have checked them.
