# The then-self and the now-self

Every memoir has two narrators: the person you were when it happened, and the person you are as you write. The book's power lives in the tension between them.

- **The then-self experiences.** They don't know how it ends. In scenes, write with their knowledge, their words and their fears. A ten-year-old doesn't think "my mother's depression", they think "Mum's on the sofa again".
- **The now-self understands.** They step forward in short passages and see the pattern: "It took me thirty years to understand she was as frightened as I was."
- **Keep them apart.** Don't let the now-self explain in the middle of a scene. Let the scene land first and reflect afterwards, ideally in a paragraph of its own.
- **Insight comes after.** An insight only rings true if the reader has seen what led to it. Show first, interpret after.
- **Let the distance grow.** Early in the book the now-self often stays in the background. Towards the end they take up more room, as understanding becomes what is left.
- **The driving question.** Put into one sentence what the now-self is trying to understand, for example "Why did I stay so long?" Every chapter should move that question forward.
- **Tense.** The usual choice is the past tense through the then-self's eyes, with the now-self in the present when they step forward: "I understand now that..." Present tense in the scenes gives closeness, but makes it harder for the now-self to be heard. Choose once and stick to it.
- **Distance first.** You can write about something you are still in the middle of, but the now-self needs some distance to see the pattern. If it isn't there yet, write the draft for yourself and wait with the book.

**In non-fiction** the then-self is the reader: the one who doesn't know yet. Write each chapter from where they stand, not where you stand. Ask what the reader believes before the chapter, and what they should believe after it.

**Try this:** write the same memory twice. First only through the then-self's eyes, then add a single sentence from the now-self at the end. Compare which version says more.
