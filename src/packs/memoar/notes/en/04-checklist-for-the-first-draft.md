# Checklist for the first draft

Read the whole draft in one go before you change anything. Then check:

**Memoir**

- Can you state the book's question in one sentence, and does every chapter touch it?
- Does the book open in a scene, not in background?
- Is the turning point written as a full scene, with place, people and dialogue?
- Can you hear both the then-self and the now-self, and are they kept apart?
- Do your own mistakes get as sharp a light as other people's?
- Is every important person more than a role, with something they do that only they would do?
- Have you tagged every fact you're unsure of with Check facts?
- Have you merged people, conversations or periods of time, and if so, does an author's note say so?
- Does the ending hold an understanding the reader has watched grow?

**Non-fiction**

- Are the reader's problem and the book's promise in the introduction?
- Does every chapter have a clear claim and at least one concrete example?
- Do the chapters build on each other without the reader having to jump?
- Does every figure, quote and claim have a source in Groundwork?
- Do you say where knowledge is uncertain or disputed?
- Is every technical term explained the first time it appears, or could it be cut?
- Can the reader do something concrete after the last chapter?

**Both**

- Have you gone through the passages tagged Sensitive, and do you know why each one is needed?
- Does anything single out a living person as criminal or blameworthy? Note it for the publisher.
- Is it a draft? Good. It isn't supposed to be finished yet.
