# Subgenres

The promises of memoir and non-fiction hold for all, but each subgenre has its own weight. Choose early: it settles who speaks, how much you may invent and what the reader should be able to do afterwards.

**The memoir**

- The promise: a bounded chapter of a life with a question and a transformation.
- Set-up: scenes with the then-self, short passages with the now-self.
- Pitfall: forgetting the question. Every scene should belong to it.

**The autobiography**

- The promise: a whole life in order, often by someone who stands for something public.
- Set-up: chronology, with a few scenes given time.
- Pitfall: a list of events. Find what binds them together.

**The autobiographical novel (autofiction)**

- The promise: a story close to a life, with freedom to shape.
- Set-up: the novel's tools on a life you know, with a clear line to the truth.
- Pitfall: real people under the cover of fiction.

**The essay**

- The promise: a thought that is tested and changes while the reader follows.
- Set-up: a concrete starting point, a turn and an answer that is not a verdict.
- Pitfall: knowing the answer from the start.

**Popular science**

- The promise: a hard subject made understandable and exciting for lay readers.
- Set-up: questions instead of facts, stories about people, pictures.
- Pitfall: simplification that turns into error.

**Self-help and how-to**

- The promise: the reader can do something more after the book.
- Set-up: a problem, a method, exercises and examples.
- Pitfall: promises the book can't keep. Say what doesn't work.

**Narrative journalism**

- The promise: an examined subject told with scenes and people.
- Set-up: one case, several voices and a red thread.
- Pitfall: losing the sources. Spell out what you know, and how.

**Ask yourself:**

- Which subgenre is mine, and which am I drifting into by accident?
- What do I promise the reader will learn or understand?
- Which question does the book ask, and why are you the one answering?
