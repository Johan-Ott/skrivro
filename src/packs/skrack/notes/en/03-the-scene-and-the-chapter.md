# The scene and the chapter

Dread is built in small units. This is how to work at the level of scenes and chapters.

**The opening.** Start in ordinary life, but with something off in the very first paragraph. No monster, just a detail: the dog refuses to enter the room, a dry flat smells of wet earth. The reader should know this is horror before the main character does.

**Build the scene in three steps.**

- **The normal.** The place, and what the character is doing.
- **The deviation.** Something small that does not fit. Let the character hesitate, explain it away and carry on.
- **The confirmation.** It returns, bigger or closer. End the scene before it is explained.

**Pace.** Short sentences and paragraphs as tension rises. Longer, calmer prose in the quiet spells. Slow down just before the worst: the door handle, the breathing, the sound on the stairs. It is the waiting that frightens.

**The senses.** Sound, smell, temperature and touch frighten more than sight. Cold coming from the wrong direction. A hand that weighs too little.

**Point of view.** A close, limited viewpoint works best: the reader knows only what the character knows and cannot see round the corner. If you switch between several characters, the reader can sometimes know more than the one in danger. Then every calm second becomes torture.

**The chapter ending.** End on a question, not an answer. A sound nobody should be able to make. A discovery that changes everything before it. A quiet ending where the reader knows something the character does not.

**Example:** She locks the front door, checks it twice and climbs into bed. From the stairwell she hears someone trying the lock, with her keys.

**Label the scenes.** Put the labels Omen, Dread, Shock, Rule and Respite on your scenes. Filter in Contents and check the rhythm, or whether three shocks stand in a row with no respite between them.
