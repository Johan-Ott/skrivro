# Reasonable choices: why do they go into the room?

The most common complaint about horror is that characters do stupid things. The reader forgives almost nothing that is only there to move the plot. What they do forgive is what they would have done themselves.

**Let the character do the reasonable thing.** They call the police, they do not go into the cellar alone, they pack and leave. Then make the reasonable thing impossible or not enough.

**Seven reasons to stay or go in.**

- **Love.** Someone they love is in there. It is the strongest reason.
- **Guilt.** They have done something, and leaving would be admitting it.
- **No one else.** Help comes too late, does not believe, or cannot come.
- **Ignorance.** They do not know what it is. The threat has not yet shown itself as a threat.
- **Price.** Leaving means losing the home, the job, the child.
- **Compulsion.** The way out is blocked by weather, an injury, a physical limit or a rule the threat itself has set.
- **Doubt.** They do not know whether it is really happening, and do not dare stand by it.

**The reasonable choice that goes wrong.** The best choice is the one that looks reasonable in the moment and turns out to be wrong. They phone a friend for support, and then the threat starts following the friend too.

**Let several make different decisions.** In a group, not everyone should do the same. One leaves, one stays, one negotiates. Then each choice costs something different, and the reader can root for different people.

**Try this:** write one line for each scene where someone goes toward the danger. "They go there because ..." If nothing resembles one of the seven reasons, the scene is a risk.
