# What kind of horror?

Before you plan the staircase, decide what the threat is and how much the reader will ever know. These choices govern everything from the pace to the ending.

**Where the threat comes from.** Most horror novels draw their threat from one of these sources, sometimes from two at once.

- **The supernatural.** Ghosts, creatures, curses. Its strength is that it breaks the laws of the world. Its weakness is that without rules it quickly turns arbitrary.
- **The human.** A stalker, a cult, a neighbor. Nothing needs explaining, and it frightens precisely because it could happen.
- **The body.** Contagion, transformation, a body that will not obey. The fear lives under the skin and gets close at once.
- **The community and the old ways.** A village, a custom, a sacrifice that everyone except the main character seems to understand. The threat is the community itself.
- **The incomprehensible.** Something so vast or alien that the mind cannot hold it. It follows rules, but they were not made for people, and understanding them is itself a harm.
- **The mind.** Is it really happening, or only in the main character's head? The fear is for their sanity as much as for their life.

**Ambiguity is a choice.** Decide whether the reader will ever learn if it is real. Keep both readings alive by planting reasons for each, and hold the verdict back until late. An ending that takes it all back, as a dream or a hoax, betrays the reader. An ending that makes both readings equally terrible rewards them.

**Three registers.** Dread is the waiting for what is coming. Terror is the moment it appears. Revulsion is what the body recoils from. Dread carries the book, terror gives it its peaks, and revulsion is strongest when used sparingly.

**How much violence.** What frightens is that someone the reader cares about is in danger, not the amount of blood. Show the aftermath, and what cannot be undone. When you do show something brutal, keep it short and give it consequences.

**How the book can end.** Victory with scars, where the main character survives but is changed. Victory at a price, where something precious was given up. The threat lives on, quietly or somewhere new. Or defeat, where the main character is lost or becomes part of the threat. Choose early, so the staircase points there, and let the last chill grow out of what the reader already knows.
