# The rhythm map: omen, dread, shock, rule and respite

Horror is rhythm. The reader cannot stand constant fear, and a shock only works if something comes before it. Draw a map of the book and see whether it breathes.

**The five beats.** They are the same as the labels in Skrivro.

- **Omen:** something is wrong, but nobody notices. A sound, a smell, a dog that refuses.
- **Dread:** it is wrong, and the character knows. They explain it away, or cannot.
- **Shock:** it shows, happens or takes someone. Short. The worst is rarely longer than half a page.
- **Rule:** the reader sees one of the threat's rules in action.
- **Respite:** calm, warmth or laughter, where the reader gets to attach to the characters.

**Some patterns that work.**

- **The staircase:** Omen, Dread, Dread, Shock, Respite. A common chapter pattern. Let the staircase grow across the book: the first shocks are small, the last irreversible.
- **The false respite:** a respite the reader thinks is safe, and which turns out to be part of the threat. Use it once or twice in the book, otherwise the reader learns never to relax.
- **The hush before:** slow down just before the shock. Longer sentences, everyday chores, a still moment. The shock comes in a short sentence.
- **The aftermath:** a shock without consequence is only an effect. Give the next scene a body that shakes, a room that looks different, a silence between two people.

**Three rules for the map.**

- Two shocks in a row with no respite between wear each other out. Put something in, however small.
- A respite longer than three scenes with no omen loses its grip.
- The biggest shock should not be the first big one. Save it, and let the map grow toward it.

**Add labels and look.** Put Omen, Dread, Shock, Rule and Respite on the scenes and filter in Contents. Read the column from top to bottom as a curve. Do you see a staircase, or a flat line? Does Rule show up somewhere before it decides the ending?

**You need not follow a map.** A quiet book can have few shocks and carry everything on dread. What matters is that you know which rhythm you have chosen, and that it changes.
