# Examples: horror that does it well

Read them as craft: look for how they do it, not what happens. The sentences are our own.

- **Dracula**, Bram Stoker (1897). Shows how letters, diaries and different voices can build threat and doubt without anyone seeing the whole picture.
- **The Haunting of Hill House**, Shirley Jackson (1959). Lets mood do the work: the dread lies in what is slightly wrong, and in how the heroine sees.
- **Rosemary's Baby**, Ira Levin (1967). Builds dread from small things in an everyday setting and a protagonist nobody takes seriously.
- **The Shining**, Stephen King (1977). Joins a place with a person's own weaknesses, so that threat and fear belong together.
- **Let the Right One In**, John Ajvide Lindqvist (2004). Sets horror in a Swedish suburb and mixes the supernatural with real wounds and loneliness.
- **Mexican Gothic**, Silvia Moreno-Garcia (2020). Makes the gothic house a place with its own logic and uses the form of the genre to write about power.

**Read like this:**

- Where is the first thing that chafes, and what is it?
- Which human fear lies under the threat?
- How many pages pass between two moments of dread, and do they grow?
