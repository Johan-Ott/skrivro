# Subgenres

Where the threat comes from is in What kind of horror. Here are the subgenres that steer tone, pace and how much the reader gets to see.

**Folk horror**

- The promise: the old, the land and a community whose customs are the threat.
- Pitfall: the guest as a mere onlooker. Let their own choices carry a price.

**Gothic horror**

- The promise: a decaying house, a family with secrets and a nature that helps threaten.
- Set-up: mood, weather and a past that weighs heavily, often in letters or diary form.
- Pitfall: prose gloomy for its own sake. Let the mood lead somewhere.

**Haunting and the haunted house**

- The promise: a place where something happened and is still happening.
- Set-up: small signs that grow, until the place seems aimed at one person.
- Pitfall: effects instead of threat. What frightens is what feels aimed.

**Cosmic horror**

- The promise: something so large the mind cannot hold it, and humanity is insignificant.
- Pitfall: describing the indescribable at length. Show consequences, not anatomy.

**Slasher**

- The promise: a hunter, a group and a fight to survive a night.
- Set-up: clear rules, pace and a person who can.
- Pitfall: victims who are just numbers. Give each one a trait.

**Psychological horror**

- The promise: the question whether it happens for real, and how far the main character can trust what they see.
- Pitfall: doubt as just an effect. Let the protagonist's reading have consequences.

**Body horror**

- The promise: the body that betrays, transforms or turns strange.
- Pitfall: disgust without meaning. Tie it to a fear of something real.

**Ask yourself:**

- Which subgenre is mine, and which am I drifting into by accident?
- What does it give me that the others don't?
- Which human fear lies beneath the threat?
