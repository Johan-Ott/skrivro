# Examples: historical novels that do it well

Read them as craft: look for how they do it, not what happens. The sentences are our own.

- **The Emigrants**, Vilhelm Moberg (1949). Shows how a great historical movement can be carried by everyday things like soil, bread and grief, and how a language can be true to its time without being pastiche.
- **The Road to Jerusalem**, Jan Guillou (1998). How a long period is made clear through one person whose eyes the reader follows, with the research lying beneath the action.
- **The Pillars of the Earth**, Ken Follett (1989). A great building project makes the period concrete, with many characters who each get a clear want.
- **Wolf Hall**, Hilary Mantel (2009). A real figure at the center, close and in the present tense, with a voice that makes the familiar strange again.
- **Hamnet**, Maggie O'Farrell (2020). How time and place are made through the senses, and how a famous name can stand in the shadow of the invented.
- **Girl with a Pearl Earring**, Tracy Chevalier (1999). How a single painting becomes a way into a whole daily life, through small details and a restrained voice.

**Read like this:**

- Which detail makes you believe the period?
- Where does history break into the characters' choices?
- How does the book handle what it doesn't know, and the author's note?
