# Checklist for the first draft

Go through the list when the draft is done. Fact checking comes last, once you know which scenes will stay.

**The story**

- Does the main character want something concrete that the age makes hard?
- Do the historical events break into their life and force choices, or do they just sit in the background?
- Is there suspense even for a reader who knows how the real events ended?
- Do the characters make decisions, or are they only struck by history?

**The age**

- Does it feel like another time from the first page?
- Is the age shown through daily life and action rather than explanation?
- Do the characters think like people of their time about children, faith, gender, class and honor?
- Have you checked travel times, letters and news? How long did it take to get between places?
- Do seasons, weekdays and the characters' ages match the timeline?

**The language**

- Are there modern words or phrases that pull the reader out of the age?
- Is the dialogue readable without sounding contemporary?
- Are the period words few enough not to become pastiche?

**Fact and fiction**

- Has every scene labelled Check facts actually been checked?
- Are dates, places and outcomes of the real events correct?
- Do real people in the text do anything that contradicts what is known about them?
- Are there true details the reader will think are wrong, and have you anchored or explained them?
- Do you have a list of everything you changed or invented, for the author's note?

**The ending**

- Are the invented characters' fates decided by their own choices?
- Does the ending say something about the age that the reader takes away?
