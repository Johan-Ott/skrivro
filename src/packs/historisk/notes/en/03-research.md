# Research

Research serves the story. You do not need to know everything about the age, but you do need to know everything your characters see, do and believe.

**Start broad, then narrow.** First read a couple of general histories to get the chronology and the big picture. Then move to scholarship on your particular subject, and last to the sources from the time itself. The overview tells you what happened. The sources give you voices, words and details.

**Sources that bring a scene to life.**

- **Letters and diaries.** How people expressed themselves, what they worried about and what they never thought worth mentioning.
- **Court records.** Quarrels, thefts and slander, with testimony that often preserves ordinary people's own words.
- **Probate inventories.** What a home actually held, from pots to clothes, and what everything was worth.
- **Parish registers and censuses.** Births, marriages, causes of death, moves, and in some places who could read.
- **Newspapers and advertisements.** Prices, news, rumors and what was new and coveted.
- **Travel accounts.** Visitors from elsewhere notice what the locals took for granted.
- **Maps and pictures.** What the place looked like, where the roads ran and how far it was.

**Objects and places.** Go to museums and open air museums, hold the objects and see how cramped people lived. Visit the place, ideally in the same season as the book, and walk the distances your characters walk. Try a task yourself if you can. Lighting a wood stove or carrying water teaches more than any description.

**Ask people.** Archivists, museum staff, local history societies and those who keep old crafts alive often know what the books leave out. Come with concrete questions.

**Read against the grain.** Most sources were written by men, by those who could write and by those in power. Women, children, servants and the poor often appear only in court records, accounts and inventories. That is where you have to look for them.

**Beware the myths.** Much that is repeated about the past is not true. Do not trust a claim because it appears in many places. Trace it back to where it came from.

**Log the source at once.** Enter every source in a source log as you read, with page references. Otherwise you will never find it again when you need it for the author's note.

**When it is enough.** Research until you can write the first scene, then write. Keep researching in rounds between drafts, and label what you do not know with Check facts instead of stopping.
