# Writing another age

The hard part of historical fiction is not knowing things. It is using them well.

**Research without the lecture.** You will know ten times more than fits on the page. Let only what the scene needs show through, and let it show through action. Do not write that women could not inherit. Let her brother take over the farm the day after the funeral while she carries out the coffee.

**The detail the character would notice.** People in their own time do not explain their daily lives. She does not think about the candle being tallow, only that it is burning low. Choose details by what the character cares about, not by what you have read.

**Period voice without pastiche.** Readability beats authenticity. Avoid modern words that pull the reader out of the age, like okay, stressed or focus, but do not write in mock old English. Use a handful of period words for objects and tasks, and let sentence rhythm do the rest. Dialogue can be a little tighter and more formal than today's.

**Anachronism of the mind.** The most dangerous mistake is not a zip in the wrong century but a character who thinks like today. How did people see children, God, illness, debt and honour? Let characters hold views that grate against your own. A hero who alone carries modern values rings false. Give them instead one small step beyond their time, with a price.

**Real events, invented people.** The safest approach is to let invented characters live in and around real events. Real figures can appear in the background, but be careful about putting words and deeds in their mouths that contradict what is known.

**The gaps are your freedom.** Where the sources are silent, invent. Where they speak clearly, follow them or account for the departure in the author's note.

**Label what you are unsure of.** Put Check facts on the scene and keep writing. Research in the middle of a scene kills the flow.
