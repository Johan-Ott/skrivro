# Tropes and devices

The trope is the promise, the execution is yours.

- **The woman who breaks the norm.** _Works when_ the norm has a price and those around her have reasons to hold to it. _Cliché when_ the norm exists so she can be the only one who sees clearly.
- **The emigrant journey.** _Works when_ the road costs and what was left behind stays visible. _Cliché when_ the new land is only the promise.
- **The family saga.** _Works when_ each generation has its own question and errs for its own reasons. _Cliché when_ it is a list of births and deaths.
- **Forbidden love.** _Works when_ the obstacle is the time's own. _Cliché when_ a secret noble birth solves everything.
- **The home front.** _Works when_ rations and letters carry the weight of war. _Cliché when_ everyone waits beautifully for someone to return.
- **Plague and famine.** _Works when_ we see how it changes relationships and choices. _Cliché when_ it only adds corpses.
- **The witch trial.** _Works when_ the accusers have reasons the reader understands. _Cliché when_ they are evil and the accused saints.
- **Meetings with the famous.** _Works when_ the meeting changes something for the invented character. _Cliché when_ the protagonist happens to be present at every famous event.
- **The letter or document.** _Works when_ the text has its own voice and gaze. _Cliché when_ it is an easy solution to the mystery.
- **The forgotten woman.** _Works when_ the gaps in the sources are freedom and you account for them. _Cliché when_ she did everything the men got credit for.

**Make it yours.** Change one condition, not the trope: which period, which class, what price. If you build the book on one trope, break it once, on purpose.
