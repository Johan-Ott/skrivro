# Subgenres

The promises of historical fiction hold for all of them, but each subgenre has its own weight. Choose early: it settles research, tone and how near real figures may stand.

**Adventure and war fiction**

- The promise: pace, danger and a time that demands courage.
- Pitfall: battles as a catalogue. Stay close to one person.

**Romance in a historical setting**

- The promise: a love the period makes harder. See The promises of romance for the couple's arc.
- Pitfall: modern ideas of love that make the obstacles unmotivated.

**The historical mystery**

- The promise: a puzzle where the period is part of the case. See The promises of crime fiction for fair play.
- Pitfall: modern technology sneaking in. Let the period have its own ways of knowing.

**The biographical novel**

- The promise: a real person's life, with the inner sides the sources lack.
- Set-up: one chapter of a life rather than all of it, and a clear line between known and invented.
- Pitfall: filling gaps as if they were fact. Account for them in the author's note.

**The family saga**

- The promise: time passes through a family, and the reader follows what is handed on.
- Pitfall: many people and no center. Choose whose hand the reader holds.

**Antiquity and the Middle Ages**

- The promise: a world that is truly strange, where belief, power and daily life differ from ours.
- Pitfall: making the strange familiar. Let it chafe.

**Alternate history**

- The promise: a what-if followed consistently. See The promises of science fiction for the logic.
- Pitfall: explaining what happened instead of what it does to people.

**Ask yourself:**

- Which subgenre is mine, and which am I drifting into by accident?
- How much research does the book carry, and where does it show?
- What does my protagonist not know about their own time that the reader does?
