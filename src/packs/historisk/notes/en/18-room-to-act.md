# Room to act: what was the person allowed to do?

A common question when writing historical fiction is how a person who was not allowed to do anything can still drive a plot. The answer is that there was almost always room to act, but it looked different from today.

**Map the room before you plan.** For each main character, write down:

- **What the person was formally allowed to do, own and decide.** The law, class, sex, age and place.
- **What the person could do informally.** The household, kin, neighbors, church, craft and market.
- **What it cost.** Reputation, security, lost help, punishment.
- **Who could give room.** A husband, a father, a priest's help, a rich relative.

**Four ways to act within the limits.**

- **Through others.** She gets her husband to do it, or gets the neighbor to speak. Power that goes through people shows in the sources more than you would think.
- **Through the edge of the rule.** A woman could often run a business as a widow when she could not as a wife. A skilled person learns where the limits lie.
- **Through the gap between rule and practice.** What the law forbade could be common in reality, and the other way round.
- **By breaking it, and paying.** Sometimes the person acts against the norm, and the price is real. That is when the reader understands what the norm cost.

**Avoid the comfortable shortcuts.**

- **The modern hero in the wrong clothes.** They want what we want, and everyone around them is stupid.
- **The one who is always right.** Give the person a mistake of their own that belongs to the time.
- **The only one without prejudice.** The prejudices of the time lived in everyone. Give them some. If they let go of them, it should cost.
- **Unfreedom that is only suffering.** People lived, loved and laughed even within narrow limits.

**Try this:** write one line: "She could not ..., but she could ..., and it cost ...". Fill it in for every big action in the book.
