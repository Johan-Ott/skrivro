# Revision in three passes (template)

Professionals don't revise from page one to the last page, fixing everything on the way. They do it in passes, from the big to the small, because there is no point polishing sentences in a scene that will be cut. Leave the manuscript alone for a few weeks first. You should read it as a stranger.

**Pass 1: The structure**

Read the whole book in one go, preferably on paper or another screen. Write nothing in the text. Write one line per chapter: what happens, and what changes.

- **Make a reverse outline.** The lines you wrote are the book's real plan, not the one you made before. Compare them. Where did the book go somewhere else, and was it better?
- **Find the middle.** Does something turn near halfway? If three chapters in a row stand in the same place, that is where the middle sags.
- **Follow every thread.** Open the main character, each side character and the B story and see in Mentioned in where they appear. A character who disappears for a hundred pages is either forgotten or unnecessary.
- **Follow the arc.** Read only the chapters where the main character makes a choice. Do the choices change?
- **Cut, merge, move.** This is when it costs the least. A chapter that changes nothing is cut or merged with another.

**Pass 2: The scenes**

Take one scene at a time, in order.

- **Whose scene is it, and what do they want?** If there is no answer, there is no scene.
- **What changes?** Read the first and last paragraph. If the situation is the same, the scene does no work.
- **Come in late, leave early.** Cut the warm-up at the start, and leave the scene when the reader has understood, not when the character has.
- **Cut the pleasantries in dialogue.** Hello, how are you, thanks, goodbye. Start the line where something is at stake.
- **Which question pulls the reader through the scene?** It need not be suspense. A question asked early and only half answered is enough: will she say it?
- **Does the chapter end on a question?** Or at a place where the reader can put the book down?
- **Are the facts right?** Names, distances, times and who knows what. Chronological order and the notes have the answers.

**Pass 3: The lines**

Only now, when everything is where it should be, do you polish the language.

- **Read aloud.** The ear finds what the eye skips: rhythm, repetitions and lines nobody would say.
- **Run Review** on every scene for repeated words and long sentences.
- **The body knows.** "Her heart pounded" and "a shiver ran down his spine" are already written. What does this particular body feel, in the hands, the stomach, the neck?
- **Beautiful must not take the place of true.** A sentence that sounds fine but does not fit the person or the moment goes, however much you like it.
- **Cut the filter words.** "She saw that", "he felt how", "she noticed". Show what she sees.
- **Cut the explanations after the line.** If the line already says it, "she said angrily" isn't needed.
- **Cut a tenth.** Almost every manuscript is better for it.

**Then**

Give the manuscript to a test reader, and ask what they felt and where they got bored, not what they think you should change. Start over at pass 1 with what you hear.
