# Pitfalls and revision

The most common pitfalls, and what to do about them.

- **Insta-love.** Attraction is not love. Let them see each other act under pressure before they fall.
- **The bond as a shortcut.** Fate says they belong together, so the book stops showing why. Give them reasons to choose each other that would hold even without the magic.
- **The misunderstanding one talk would fix.** Give the conflict a real cause: an oath, a duty, a secret that would get someone killed if it came out.
- **Magic without a price.** If the power always works, the tension drains away. Make it cost memory, blood, years or someone they love.
- **Worldbuilding in a lump.** Three pages of history before anything happens. Break it up and let it arrive when it is needed.
- **The passive lead.** They must choose and act, not just be rescued or carried along by fate.
- **The chosen one with no edges.** Strongest of all, beautiful without knowing it and flawless in their goodness. Give your lead flaws that cost, and let others be right about them sometimes.
- **A love interest who is only dangerous.** Darkness is not enough. Show the tenderness, the humour and why they are worth the risk.
- **Cruelty mistaken for chemistry.** Taunts and threats can create friction, but if one hurts the other and never takes responsibility, it stops being romantic. Show respect growing.
- **Forgiveness for free.** After the betrayal, an apology and a kiss are not enough. Whoever betrayed must show remorse in deeds, and the other must get to choose forgiveness at their own pace.
- **The fantasy as a backdrop.** The war is mentioned but never forces a decision. Let the threat change what the couple can do, where they can go and whom they can trust.
- **The romance vanishes in the final battle.** The grand gesture belongs in the battle, not in a chapter afterwards.

**Revision, in three passes**

Pass 1, the structure:

- Follow only the love arc through the book: tag the scenes Spark and see if the steps rise.
- Follow only the world arc: tag Magic and Power and see if the threat grows.
- Read the midpoint and the ending side by side: does the ending answer what the midpoint opened?
- Read the scenes tagged Betrayal and what follows them: does the reconciliation get as much room as the break-up?

Pass 2, the scenes:

- Find every scene tagged Secret and check that the betrayal has its roots there.
- Read every chapter ending: is there a hook?
- Cut every explanation of the world the reader can do without.

Pass 3, the lines:

- Read the chapters tagged Second POV on their own: does the voice sound unlike the lead's, even without names?
