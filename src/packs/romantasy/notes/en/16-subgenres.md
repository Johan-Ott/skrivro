# Subgenres

The promises of romantasy hold for all of them, but the weight shifts. Choose early: it settles the heat, the tone and how large the world needs to be.

**Fae romantasy**

- The promise: an alluring and dangerous people with their own laws, bargains and courts.
- Set-up: words are weapons, price and promise are linked, and a human is a guest in a strange world.
- Pitfall: superhuman lovers with no weakness of their own. Give the power resistance.

**War academy and trial**

- The promise: a school where danger is real, and rivalry turns into love.
- Set-up: rules, rankings and tests that drive the chapters.
- Pitfall: the school as scenery. Let the rules and tests press on the couple.

**Court romantasy**

- The promise: power, intrigue and vows in a castle.
- Set-up: conversations as moves in a game, alliances that shift.
- Pitfall: intrigue without consequences. Let betrayal cost.

**Cozy romantasy**

- The promise: low stakes, warmth, a small place and a slow love.
- Set-up: daily life, craft and community carry the book, and the magic is small.
- Pitfall: forgetting the opposition. Even cozy needs something to win.

**Urban romantasy**

- The promise: the hidden world in our city, and a couple living in both.
- Set-up: the city as a character, two lives that chafe, often with a puzzle.
- Pitfall: a secret nobody questions. Explain why nobody knows.

**Fairy-tale retelling**

- The promise: a familiar tale that looks anew at its hero and its villain.
- Set-up: recognize the shape, change who holds the power and what is true.
- Pitfall: following the tale so closely that nothing surprises.

**Dark romantasy**

- The promise: violence, betrayal and a love pressed by them. The reader wants the darkness.
- Set-up: content warnings up front, clear consent in the scene.
- Pitfall: darkness replacing the relationship. Both must still choose.

**Ask yourself:**

- Which subgenre is mine, and which am I drifting into by accident?
- How large does the world need to be to carry the couple?
- What does love cost in this world, and who pays?
