# Examples: romantasy that does it well

Read them as craft: look for how they do it, not what happens. The sentences are our own.

- **Throne of Glass**, Sarah J. Maas (2012). Shows how a trial can drive the chapters and hold several threads around one heroine.
- **A Court of Thorns and Roses**, Sarah J. Maas (2015). Builds a strange world with its own rules where bargains and words carry weight, and where the relationship is shaped by them.
- **Uprooted**, Naomi Novik (2015). Anchors the magic in landscape and plot, so that love and world shape each other.
- **The Cruel Prince**, Holly Black (2018). Makes the power games of a court the obstacle itself, so that every conversation has a stake.
- **From Blood and Ash**, Jennifer L. Armentrout (2020). Promises and pays out heat in steps, with secrets planted early.
- **Fourth Wing**, Rebecca Yarros (2023). Creates pace from short chapters, trials and a world where danger never rests.

**Read like this:**

- How early do you understand what kind of book it is?
- Where does the world give the couple an obstacle, and where does it give them something?
- Which chapter pays out the first of the promises?
