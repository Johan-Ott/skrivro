# The promises of romantasy

A reader who picks up romantasy is buying two promises at once. Break either one and the book feels like a betrayal, however good the rest is.

- **Love carries the book.** The relationship is one of two main plots, not a subplot. Take it out and the fantasy plot should collapse.
- **A happy ending, or a promise.** The couple choose each other: for ever, or for now with a promise that holds until the next book in the series. A couple parted for good breaks the genre.
- **A world that costs.** The magic has rules and a price, and the powers around them create the real obstacles to love.
- **Heat that is earned.** Tension builds in steps: resistance, being forced together, respect, closeness, almost. The reader should wait, but never wait for nothing.
- **Two people with a will of their own.** Both want something, make their own choices and have something to lose. Neither is just a prize for the other.
- **Consent on the page.** Even in a dark, dangerous world it is clear that both want this. Danger and power can charge the space between them, but closeness is a choice.
- **Stakes on two levels.** What is at stake in the world is also at stake between them. When the realm shakes, so does the bond.

**Promise early.** By the first chapters the reader should know what kind of book this is: how dark, how steamy, how epic. If the first scene shows an execution in the square and a look between two enemies in the crowd, you have promised both danger and heat. Keep that promise, in the same tone, all the way.

**The heat level.** Decide how much of the sensual is shown, from closed door to open, and stay there. A sudden leap in either direction feels like a different book.

**The series.** If the book ends happy for now, the reader still needs emotional closure: the couple have chosen each other, even if the war goes on.
