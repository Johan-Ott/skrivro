# Examples: crime novels that do it well

Read them as craft: look for how they do it, not what happens. The sentences are our own.

- **The Murder of Roger Ackroyd**, Agatha Christie (1926). A textbook case of fair play: everything the reader needs to solve the puzzle is on the page.
- **Roseanna**, Maj Sjöwall and Per Wahlöö (1965). A case solved with patience and routine, a team instead of a genius and a society glimpsed behind the crime.
- **Faceless Killers**, Henning Mankell (1991). The crime as a mirror of its time, and a detective whose weariness and doubt belong to the puzzle.
- **The Ice Princess**, Camilla Läckberg (2003). A small town that decides which secrets can be carried, and a private life woven in without losing the puzzle.
- **In the Woods**, Tana French (2007). Shows what a case costs the detective, and how much a voice can carry.
- **The Thursday Murder Club**, Richard Osman (2020). Amateurs with reasons of their own, humor that carries and a puzzle that is still held strict.

**Read like this:**

- Where do you first meet the culprit?
- Which is the first clue you should have seen?
- What does the case cost the detective?
