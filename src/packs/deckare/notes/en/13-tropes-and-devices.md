# Tropes and devices

A trope is a device the reader recognises and wants. The trope is the promise, the execution is yours.

- **The closed circle.** No one gets in or out. _Works when_ the place shapes what each person can hide. _Cliché when_ the phone line is cut and nobody wonders why no one calls.
- **The locked room.** The crime seems impossible. _Works when_ the solution is simple and every piece was visible. _Cliché when_ it needs an impossible mechanism.
- **The second murder.** The middle gets pace. _Works when_ the victim changes the picture of the first. _Cliché when_ it is just more bodies.
- **The false solution.** The detective is wrong in the middle. _Works when_ the mistake is reasonable and costs something. _Cliché when_ no one believed it.
- **The red herring.** A false trail. _Works when_ it is true and has its own explanation. _Cliché when_ it is a trick that gets taken back.
- **The witness who sees too much.** _Works when_ they are in danger precisely because they don't understand. _Cliché when_ they die before they can talk.
- **The unreliable narrator.** _Works when_ everything said is true but was read wrong. _Cliché when_ the reveal is a diagnosis instead of a choice.
- **The broken detective.** _Works when_ the flaw shapes the method and costs something. _Cliché when_ it is a label instead of a wound.
- **The local amateur.** _Works when_ they have their own reason and knowledge the police lack. _Cliché when_ the police are fools so that they can shine.
- **The gathering.** All the suspects in one room. _Works when_ something is at stake in the room. _Cliché when_ it is a lecture and the culprit confesses at once.
- **The case turns personal.** _Works when_ it makes the investigation harder: judgement clouds, they see too little. _Cliché when_ it only raises the stakes.
- **The killer's chapters in italics.** _Works when_ the voice gives the reader something the detective lacks. _Cliché when_ it only exists to frighten.
- **The cold case.** _Works when_ time is part of the puzzle: what have people forgotten, moved, kept quiet? _Cliché when_ new technology solves it overnight.

**Make it yours.** Change one condition, not the trope: who investigates, what it costs, where it happens. If you build the book on one trope, break it once, on purpose.
