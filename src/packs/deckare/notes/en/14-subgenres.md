# Subgenres

The promises of crime fiction hold for all of them, but each subgenre has its own weight. Choose early: it settles your detective, your tone and how violence is shown.

**The classic puzzle**

- The promise: a closed circle and a riddle that can be solved. The reader competes with the detective.
- Set-up: few suspects, a clear timeline, the reveal as the high point.
- Pitfall: characters who are chess pieces. Give each one their own lie and their own need.

**The cosy mystery**

- The promise: an amateur, a small community, violence offstage, a world that heals.
- Set-up: setting and supporting cast carry the book as much as the puzzle.
- Pitfall: forgetting the puzzle. Cosy without fair play is just small talk.

**The small-town series**

- The promise: a known place, police or amateur, and relationships that run through several books.
- Set-up: the place and daily life carry the series, the case is each book.
- Pitfall: the place as backdrop. Give it a problem of its own that the case touches.

**The police procedural**

- The promise: credible police work, a team and often several viewpoints.
- Set-up: routine is part of the tension: interviews, surveillance, paperwork, fights over priorities.
- Pitfall: jargon as padding. One right detail beats ten.

**The dark mystery (nordic noir)**

- The promise: the crime as a mirror of a society, a place or the detective.
- Set-up: slow mood, a social eye, a private life at stake.
- Pitfall: gloom as a pose. The heaviness needs a reason in the story.

**The psychological mystery**

- The promise: why matters more than who. The reader gets close to the culprit or the victim.
- Set-up: often several voices, and the reveal rests on something inner.
- The line to the thriller: a mystery asks who, a thriller asks whether anyone survives. If the book mainly asks the second, it belongs in Thriller.
- Pitfall: hiding so much that fair play is lost.

**The historical mystery**

- The promise: time is part of the puzzle. Methods, laws and daily life make the case harder.
- Set-up: conversation and observation as tools, not modern technology.
- Pitfall: a modern person in costume. Let the detective believe what the time believed.

**The legal mystery**

- The promise: the truth is tested in court and the proof has to hold. Pitfall: law as lecture, so make it drama.

**Ask yourself:**

- Which subgenre is mine, and which am I drifting into by accident?
- Which promise weighs most here?
- Who does the reader guess at the midpoint, and is that okay?
