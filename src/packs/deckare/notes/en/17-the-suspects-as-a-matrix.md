# The suspects

A puzzle needs several people who could have done it. The reader wants to guess, change the guess and guess again. So build the circle deliberately, so no one is there only to fill space.

**Three questions for each suspect.**

- **Motive:** what do they win or save? Money, reputation, love, security or revenge.
- **Opportunity:** could they have been there, had the key, known when?
- **Secret:** what do they hide that has nothing to do with the murder, but makes them behave suspiciously?

Give every suspect a secret of their own. Then you have several ways to draw out lies, and several ways to make the reader guess wrong.

**Four kinds of suspect.** Mix them in the circle.

- **The obvious one.** Has the strongest motive and the weakest alibi. The reader suspects them first, and the book clears them in the middle.
- **The surprising one.** Seems harmless but has a hidden reason.
- **The one who protects someone.** Lies to save another, and so hides the truth.
- **The culprit.** Suspected once, early, and cleared by something that later proves false.

**Share the attention.** If one person gets twice as many pages as the others, the reader points at them, one way or the other. Count scenes and give the culprit a reasonable place, neither too large nor too small.

**Let suspects take turns.** Let each be the most suspected in one part of the book, and then be dismissed by a clue. Then the reader's suspicion shifts and the middle carries.

**Avoid padding.** If a person has none of the three, cut them or give them something. A suspect who exists only to be a candidate feels like a candidate.

**In Skrivro.** Make a note The suspect for each person, and mark scenes with Suspect. Find the person few scenes touch: they are either unimportant or hidden too well.

**Try this:** write one sentence per suspect: "They could have done it because ..." If you can't, they are not a suspect yet.
