# Checklist for the first draft

Run through this when the draft is done, before you start polishing sentences. The puzzle has to hold first.

**The crime and the question**

- Is the crime discovered within the first tenth of the book?
- Is the question that drives the book clear by chapter three at the latest?

**The culprit**

- Has the reader met the culprit within the first third?
- Do motive, means and opportunity survive close inspection?
- Is it their own mistake, not chance, that gives them away?
- Do they do something during the investigation that points at them, but can be explained away in the moment?
- Is the seed of the motive in the text long before the reveal?

**The clues**

- Does every clue the solution relies on appear before the detective understands it?
- Could an attentive reader have solved the puzzle from what is on the page alone?
- Are the clues spread across the book rather than gathered at the end?
- Does every red herring have its own explanation that gets resolved?

**The suspects**

- Do at least three people have motive, opportunity and something to hide?
- Does each have a distinct trait, so the reader does not mix them up?
- Are they cleared one at a time, for good reasons?

**Time**

- Does the timeline add up, minute by minute around the crime?
- Does When on the chapters match What really happened?
- Do the alibis hold or break in the right places?

**The detective**

- Do they solve the case through their own insight?
- Does their work match how police, prosecutors and courts actually operate where the book is set?
- Does the case cost them something personal?
- Do they understand something about themselves at the end that they did not at the start?

**The solution**

- Does the reveal happen in a scene, with the culprit present and something at stake?
- Is the explanation afterwards short, covering only what the reader has not already grasped?
- Does the last chapter show what the case cost?
