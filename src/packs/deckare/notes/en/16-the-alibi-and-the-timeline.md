# The alibi and the timeline

A case falls apart when the time does not add up. The reader can forgive many things, but not something that happened at eight and also at half past. Build the timeline before you write, and keep it updated.

**Begin with the truth.** Write down minute by minute what really happened, in What really happened. That is the answer key. Everything else is what the characters said they did.

**Three columns for each suspect.**

- **What they say:** where they were, and with whom.
- **What holds:** the parts that can be confirmed.
- **What is a lie, and why:** what does not hold, and the reason. The reason is rarely the murder.

**Make the alibi a clue.** An alibi is not only an obstacle. When it cracks, it is a discovery. Think of three kinds of flaw.

- **A hole:** a period no one can confirm.
- **A contradiction:** two people give different times for the same thing.
- **Too good:** an alibi so detailed it must have been rehearsed.

**Time in the text.** Write times and days often, but vary how: the clock on the wall, a schedule, a remark like "it was before the news". The reader should be able to count along without having to.

**Seasons and light.** Sunset, the last bus, when the shop closes and who can get there. Small practical facts are the best tools for making an alibi fall.

**In Skrivro.** Fill in When on the chapters and sort in Chronological order. Mark scenes with Alibi. Are there two scenes where someone is in different places at the same time? Then you have either a clue or a mistake, and you want to know which.

**Try this:** draw a timeline of the night of the murder with each person's movements. How many minutes is no one seen? Those are the openings of the puzzle.
