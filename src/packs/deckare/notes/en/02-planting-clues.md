# Planting clues

A clue should be seen without shining.

- **Hide it in plain sight.** Put it in a sentence about something else, or in the middle of a list.
- **Three times.** An important clue often shows three times: once in passing, once when it raises questions, once when it is explained.
- **Red herrings must be honest.** A red herring points the wrong way, but what the text says is true.
- **Label the scenes.** Put the label Clue or Red herring on the scenes where they show. Then you can filter them in Contents and see that they are fairly spread.
- **Follow the time.** Fill in When on the chapters and use Time order in Contents, so that alibis and events add up.
